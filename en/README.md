## About This Book

In the summer of 2025, one number flooded my social feed: 95%.

A report out of MIT said that over the past three years, enterprises worldwide had burned thirty to forty billion dollars on generative AI, and 95% of those projects had failed to produce any value you could write onto a financial statement. At almost the same moment, another piece of news was racing in the opposite direction: on Silicon Valley job boards, postings for a role called "Forward Deployed Engineer" (FDE) had grown eightfold in nine months. OpenAI was hiring. Anthropic was hiring. More than a hundred startups in the YC incubator were hiring.

On one side, a 95% mortality rate for enterprise AI projects; on the other, an 800% surge in demand for a single role. Put the two headlines side by side and the answer isn't hard to guess: **models are no longer scarce; the people who can wedge a model into a customer's real business are.**

That's what gave me such a strong interest in the FDE role. Years ago, when I wrote *Growth Hacker*, I was essentially doing the same thing I'm doing now: taking something that was already happening in Silicon Valley but didn't yet have a name back home, studying it systematically, and explaining it honestly. This time I used the same plodding method — combing through every piece of primary material I could find: early Palantir executives dissecting the past on podcasts, memoirs from former employees, venture-capital industry analyses, the original text of that MIT report, dozens of job postings from various companies, compensation reports, practitioners venting on forums, and the field notes of the first wave of practitioners back home.

This book is the complete distillation of that research process. It settles three questions:

- **What an FDE is**: how this role — grown out of Palantir's intelligence projects — is defined, and why it exploded in the age of AI
- **How to do it**: following the full arc of a real delivery — finding the right problem, winning the customer, activating the deployment, defending the renewal, growing the revenue, and scaling by replication
- **Who's doing it**: 112 real, verifiable cases, from Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic to Harvey and Sierra, all the way to the first wave of practitioners in China

Every figure and case in the book has its source marked in Appendix C. The act of compiling it was itself the learning; I've done my best to make every citation hold up to scrutiny. If I've missed anything, corrections via Issue are welcome.

## Why It's Free and Open

This book is, first of all, a set of study notes I wrote for myself. When you're researching an unfamiliar field, the best way to check your work is to turn it into a book someone else can understand — the parts you can't explain clearly are exactly the parts you don't yet understand.

Once it was compiled, locking it away on a hard drive felt like a bit of a waste. The FDE is still a role that has only just acquired a name in China, and much of the discussion is still stuck on "isn't this just pre-sales with a new label?" If this distillation can help an engineer considering a career switch see the full shape of the role, help a founder betting on enterprise AI avoid a few wrong turns, or help anyone trying to figure out "how does AI actually land inside an enterprise" save a few dozen hours of searching — then it's plainly worth more out in the open than sitting on my hard drive.

The value of knowledge lies in its flow, not in its hoarding.
