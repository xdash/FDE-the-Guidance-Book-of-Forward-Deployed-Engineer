## About This Book

In the summer of 2025, my WeChat Moments was flooded with the same number: 95%.

A report from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology stated that over the past three years, global enterprises burned 30 to 40 billion dollars on generative AI, and 95% of those projects failed to generate any value that could be written into financial statements. Almost at the same time, another piece of news was running wildly in the opposite direction: on Silicon Valley's job hunting websites, the postings for a position called "Forward Deployed Engineer" (FDE) had grown eightfold in nine months. OpenAI is hiring, Anthropic is hiring, and over a hundred startups in the YC incubator are hiring.

On one side is the 95% failure rate of enterprise AI projects, and on the other is an 800% surge in demand for a single role. Looking at these two pieces of news together, the answer is not hard to guess: **Models are no longer scarce; people who can integrate models into customers' real businesses are the ones who are scarce.**

This sparked my deep interest in the FDE role. Years ago, when I wrote *Growth Hacker*, what I did was essentially the same as this time: taking something that is happening in Silicon Valley but does not yet have a proper name domestically, systematically studying it, and honestly explaining it clearly. This time, I also used the same painstaking method—going through all the first-hand materials I could find: retrospectives by early Palantir executives on podcasts, memoirs of former employees, industry analyses by venture capitalists, the original text of the MIT report, dozens of job postings from various companies, salary reports, complaints from practitioners on forums, and the experience talks of the first batch of practitioners in China.

This book is the complete distillation of that research process. It clearly discusses three things:

- **What is an FDE**: How this role, which grew out of Palantir's intelligence projects, is defined, and why it has exploded in the AI era
- **How to do it**: Following the complete journey of a real delivery—finding the right problem, winning customers, activating deployment, securing renewals, expanding revenue, and scaling and replication
- **Who is doing it**: 112 verifiable real cases, from Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic to Harvey, Sierra, and the first batch of practitioners in China

All the data and cases in the book have their sources indicated in Appendix C. The process of organizing it is itself learning; I tried to make every citation verifiable; if there are any omissions, please feel free to correct them via Issues.

## Why Open it for Free

This book is, first and foremost, a set of learning notes written for myself. When researching an unfamiliar field, the best way to verify learning is to explain it in a book that others can also read and understand—the parts you cannot explain clearly are the areas you haven't fully understood yourself.

After organizing it, locking it up on a hard drive would be somewhat a pity. In China, FDE is still a role that barely has a name, and many discussions still linger on "is it just presales with a different name". If this distillation can help engineers considering a transition see the full picture of this role, help entrepreneurs engaging in enterprise AI avoid detours, and save anyone wanting to understand "how AI can actually land in enterprises" dozens of hours of search time, then publishing it openly is obviously more valuable than lying on my hard drive.

The value of knowledge lies in circulation, not in hoarding.
