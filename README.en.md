## About This Book

In the summer of 2025, one number kept showing up in my social feed: 95%.

An MIT report found that over the past three years, global enterprises burned through $30–40 billion on generative AI, and 95% of those projects failed to produce any value that showed up on a financial statement. Around the same time, another story was racing in the opposite direction: on Silicon Valley job boards, postings for a role called "Forward Deployed Engineer" (FDE) had grown eightfold in nine months. OpenAI was hiring. Anthropic was hiring. More than a hundred startups in YC's portfolio were hiring.

On one side, a 95% failure rate for enterprise AI projects. On the other, an 800% surge in demand for a single job title. Put these two facts side by side and the answer isn't hard to guess: **models are no longer scarce — the people who can actually embed a model into a customer's real business are.**

That's how I got drawn into the FDE role. Years ago, when I wrote *Growth Hacker*, I did essentially the same thing: I systematically studied something that was already happening in Silicon Valley but didn't yet have a name back home, and tried to explain it honestly. This time I used the same old-fashioned method — digging through every primary source I could find: podcast retrospectives from early Palantir executives, memoirs from former employees, VC industry analyses, the original MIT report, dozens of job postings from various companies, compensation reports, practitioner complaints on forums, and accounts from the first wave of Chinese practitioners.

This book is the complete record of that research process. It covers three things:

- **What FDE is**: how this role, which grew out of Palantir's intelligence work, is defined, and why it's exploding in the AI era
- **How it's done**: following the full journey of a real deployment — finding the right problem, winning the customer, activating deployment, retaining renewal, expanding revenue, and scaling replication
- **Who's doing it**: 112 real, verifiable cases, from Palantir, OpenAI, and Anthropic to Harvey, Sierra, and the first wave of practitioners in China

Every piece of data and every case in this book is sourced in Appendix C. The process of compiling it was itself a learning exercise, and I've tried to make every citation hold up to scrutiny; if you spot an error, please raise it via an Issue.

## Why It's Free

This book started as my own study notes. The best way to verify you've understood an unfamiliar field is to turn it into a book that someone else can understand — wherever you can't explain something clearly, that's exactly where you haven't understood it yourself.

Once it was done, it felt wasteful to just lock it away on my hard drive. FDE is still a newly-named role in China, and much of the discussion still gets stuck on "isn't this just presales with a new name?" If this material can help engineers considering a career shift see the full shape of this role, help entrepreneurs building enterprise AI avoid some detours, or save anyone trying to figure out "how does AI actually land in an enterprise" dozens of hours of searching — then publishing it clearly does more good than leaving it on my hard drive.

The value of knowledge lies in its circulation, not in hoarding it.
